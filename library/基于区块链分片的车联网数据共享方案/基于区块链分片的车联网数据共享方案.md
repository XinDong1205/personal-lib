Abstract—In recent years, the adoption of blockchain tech-
nology has signiﬁcantly advanced distributed trust and privacy
protection, rendering it a popular choice for data sharing
scenarios within the Internet of Vehicles (IoV). However, when
applied to data sharing contexts, the technology encounters
issues related to low throughput and sluggish consensus speed.
To address these issues, this paper proposes a novel data sharing
scheme tailored for IoV that leverages a sharded blockchain to
boost system throughput. The scheme dynamically selects nodes
and shards based on vehicle reputation, computing power, and
location centrality, ensuring the security of shard veriﬁcation.
Additionally, the scheme incorporates a proxy node selection
strategy and utilizes Boneh-Lynn-Shacham (BLS) signature
aggregation technology to optimize the Practical Byzantine Fault
Tolerance (PBFT) consensus mechanism. Experimental results
demonstrate that, compared with other schemes, the proposed
approach achieves superior levels of security, reliability while
maintaining lower communication complexity.
Index Terms—Internet of vehicles, data sharing, blockchain,
dynamic sharding, consensus mechanism
I. INTRODUCTION
With the rapid advancement of the Internet of Vehicles
(IoV), data security concerns have become increasingly sig-
niﬁcant [1]. The risks associated with false data and privacy
breaches pose signiﬁcant threats to the security and efﬁciency
of data sharing. While data encryption/decryption techniques
(e.g., via chaotic secure communication [2],[3], data coding
[4],[5], etc.) can ensure the secure transmission and storage
of information in vehicular networks, their algorithmic im-
plementations are complex and rely on high-quality channels
with limited anti-interference capabilities. In this situation,
there is a need for a method that can both protect data
security and cope with the complexity of the algorithms
and the limitations of channel quality. This underscores the
importance of ensuring the privacy, authenticity, and integrity
of IoV data.
In recent years, blockchain has emerged as an effective so-
lution to data privacy and trust issues due to its decentralized,
distributed, anonymous, and tamper-resistant characteristics,
making it a popular choice for data sharing scenarios in
IoV [6]. Most of the existing literature deploys a single
layer of blockchain at the RSUs level. While this design
strategy simpliﬁes the system architecture, it also introduces
some issues [7], [8], [9]. Firstly, the system throughput
decreases as the number of network nodes increases. This
is because all transactions in the blockchain system need to
be veriﬁed and stored on all nodes, and as the number of
nodes increases, system performance and efﬁciency decrease.
Secondly, existing solutions do not restrict the joining and
exiting of nodes, which provides the possibility for malicious
nodes to launch attacks. In P2P networks, nodes can freely
join and leave, which provides a possibility for malicious
nodes, they may launch collusion attacks or eclipse attacks,
affecting the normal operation of the blockchain [10].
To address these challenges, this paper proposes a se-
cure and reliable data sharing scheme built upon sharded
blockchain technology. The adoption of blockchain sharding
is driven by the necessity for high transaction throughput, a
critical requirement for handling real-time, large-scale data
generated and utilized within vehicular networks. By subdi-
viding the network into smaller pieces, known as “shards”,
each capable of independently processing its own transactions
and smart contracts, we signiﬁcantly enhance the scalability
of our solution.
In addition to scalability improvement, maintaining robust-
ness of the consensus mechanism, especially against Sybil
attacks, is equally signiﬁcant. Traditional Byzantine Fault
Tolerance (BFT) systems, while robust, have high com-
munication overheads and are susceptible to Sybil attacks.
PBFT is a consensus algorithm ensuring agreement among
nodes in distributed systems, even with up to (n −1)/3
faulty or malicious nodes. In view of this, we introduce an
optimized BLS-PBFT consensus algorithm. BLS signatures
[11] are short and easy to verify, reducing validation time and
communication overhead, while enhancing resistance to Sybil
attacks. This By combining the robustness of PBFT and the
efﬁciency of BLS signature aggregation, the overall security
and efﬁciency of our solution can be improved.
The main contributions of this work can be summarized as
follows:
• We propose a sharded blockchain-based data sharing
scheme, enhancing data security and reliability.
• We propose a blockchain sharding protocol and a proxy
node selection algorithm, reducing risks associated with
malicious nodes and improving overall system security.
• We introduce a BLS-PBFT consensus algorithm for
IoV scenarios that improves transaction veriﬁcation efﬁ-
ciency, reduces communication overhead, and increases
Sybil attack.

B. Improved PBFT Consensus for IoV
In the context of the Internet of Vehicles (IoV), consensus
algorithms play a vital role in upholding the integrity and
reliability of trafﬁc-related data. The PBFT consensus algo-
rithm stands out prominently due to its robustness against
Byzantine faults. For example, Diallo et al. [13] improved
the PBFT consensus protocol for vehicular networks for better
security and efﬁciency. Xu et al. [14] proposed a SG-PBFT
consensus protocol for the IoV, improving the traditional
PBFT algorithm by using a score grouping mechanism for
better consensus efﬁciency. Kumar et al. [15] proposed an
HN-mPBFT protocol for the IoV, streamlining PBFT con-
sensus phases to minimize message overhead and transaction
latency. The complexity of these improved PBFT algorithms
are O(n2), limiting their suitability for high-throughput, fast-
paced IoV scenarios. In this work, we propose a lightweight
BLS-PBFT approach which reduces its complexity to O(n),
better accommodating high-speed IoV environments.
III. SYSTEM MODEL
A. System Model
In our considered IoV data sharing network (as illustrated
in Fig. 1), there are three main entities: trusted authority
(TA), roadside units (RSUs), and vehicles. To ensure system
security, RSUs communicate with the TA and with each other
via wired transmission. Additionally, all RSUs and vehicles
must register with the TA prior to joining the network. The
roles and functions of these three entities are described in
details as follows.
Trusted Authority (TA): The TA serves as an authoritative
third party responsible for verifying and authorizing the
identity and conduct of vehicles and RSUs. Its primary role
is to ensure the security and integrity of data transmission
within the network. Typically, the TA is an entity designated
by the government or other organizations, entrusted with the
core task of upholding the security and trustworthiness of the
vehicular network.
RSUs: The RSU is an infrastructure device deployed along-
side roadways, designed for collecting and relaying informa-
tion sent by vehicles and TA. It also actively participates in
global blockchain consensus process and maintain storage of
all shard blockchain data.
Vehicles: Vehicles in the data sharing system have com-
putational resources, storage, and wireless communication,
enabling them to collect, process, validate, and transmit
data. However, their trustworthiness may vary as they could
potentially forge identities or provide false data for rewards.
B. Blockchain Model
In our scheme, vehicles initially perform local data process-
ing before employing the blockchain for intra-shard consensus
on the data model. Data models that meet the required
criteria are then aggregated into a global model for upload ,
which is subsequently uploaded to Roadside Units (RSUs) for
comprehensive validation. The blockchain consensus model
encompasses four distinct roles: master nodse, global node,
proxy nodes, and validator nodes. Speciﬁcally, the master
node corresponds to the TA, global nodes corresponds to
RSUs, while the proxy and validator nodes are comprised
of the vehicles themselves.
1) Master node: Tasked with inspecting and managing
node statuses, the master node allocates roles to network
nodes based on sharding and proxy selection algorithms,
assigning them to their respective consensus shards.
2) Global node: Global nodes, primarily composed of
Roadside Units (RSUs), are responsible for validating shard
blockchain data. They also synchronize and store all the data
from these shard blockchains.
3) Proxy node: Proxy nodes collect feedback results from
shard veriﬁcation nodes, generate the intra-shard block, and
disseminate it to the global nodes.
4) Validator node: Validator nodes receive unveriﬁed data
blocks from proxy nodes and verify their compliance with
the data collection criteria. They also monitor the activities
of proxy nodes.

data consensus veriﬁcation, which was tested for traditional
PBFT [12], SG-PBFT [14], and the BLS-PBFT proposed
in this paper, and conducted a comparative analysis. Fig.
5 shows the communication delay of the three different
blockchain consensus algorithms under varying numbers of
vehicles. The experiments were repeated 1000 times, and the
average time delay was recorded to improve the credibility
of the result data. It can be seen from the ﬁgure that the
BLS-PBFT consensus proposed in this paper outperforms
traditional PBFT and SG-PBFT consensus signiﬁcantly, espe-
cially as the number of vehicles increases. This improvement
can be mainly attributed to the utilization of BLS signature
aggregation technology in the prepare and commit phases.
C. Throughput
We evaluated the sharding scheme’s throughput, comparing
the rates without sharding [9] and with multi-sharding [8].
Vehicles sent 500 data models per second. After 100 blocks,
we compared the average transactions per second (TPS) under
different numbers of vehicles.
As shown in Fig. 6, our results demonstrated that our
scheme outachieves both non-sharding and multi-sharding
schemes in terms of TPS. This superiority is attributed to the
use of the BLS-PBFT consensus algorithm in our scheme,
which streamlines the consensus process, and the use of
CS sharding signiﬁcantly enhances the system’s average
consensus veriﬁcation efﬁciency. While the multi-sharding
scheme does boost system throughput, the additional latency
brought about by its multi-round inter-shard communications
offsets some of its advantages.
As shown in Fig. 7, our proposed BLS-PBFT consensus
algorithm outperforms the SG-PBFT [14] and HN-mPBFT
[15] algorithms in high-speed scenarios. This is because that
the use of BLS aggregated signature algorithm can reduce
communication complexity and signiﬁcantly enhance system
throughput.

VI. CONCLUSION
This paper proposes a blockchain sharding algorithm aimed
at enhancing data sharing efﬁciency upholding upholding data
security within the dynamic IoV environment. The algorithm
achieves this by segmenting consensus resources and trans-
forming the traditional single consensus unit into multiple
decentralized consensus units, leveraging factors such as
reputation and computing power. Speciﬁc roles are allocated
to nodes based on a proxy node selection strategy, resulting
in increased reliability and reduced view switching. Addi-
tionally, Boneh-Lynn-Shacham (BLS) signature technology
is incorporated to address communication challenges within
alliance chain. Experimental results demonstrate superior
security, reliability, and reduced communication complexity
compared to alternative data sharing schemes.
In the future, we will explore integrating this sharding
mechanism with edge computing to further enhance its scal-
ability in large-scale IoV.
