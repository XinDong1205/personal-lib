Abstract—With the rapid development of vehicular ad-hoc net-
works (VANETs) and the increasing diversiﬁcation of user de-
mands, interactions between different management domains have
becomemorefrequent.Identityauthenticationisaneffectivewayto
establish cross-domain trust and secure communication. However,
the existing cross-domain authentication schemes of VANETs are
limited to the same management or authentication technology
for each domain and rely on centralized cross-domain identity
management. Even distributed management solutions encounter
latency sensitivity, security and privacy challenges. To address
these challenges, we propose a blockchain-assisted revocable cross-
domain authentication scheme for VANETs. The proposed scheme
can establish trust between domain entities by deploying different
authenticationmethodsandusingdistributedmanagementtoavoid
single-point failures. In addition, the scheme can revoke the identity
of malicious vehicles by updating the group public key, thereby en-
suring the security and privacy of cross-domain Vehicle-to-Vehicle
(V2V) and Vehicle-to-Infrastructure (V2I) communication. This
design avoids the additional impacts of blockchain technology
constraints on the high mobility and real-time requirements of
VANETs. Security analysis and performance evaluation show that
our scheme can resist more attacks and has better security than
other related schemes while also achieving a better balance between
communication and computational cost.

In VANETs, the primary communication types are Vehicle-
to-Vehicle (V2V), Vehicle-to-Infrastructure (V2I), Vehicle-to-
Network (V2N), and Vehicle-to-Pedestrian (V2P). In-vehicle
on-board units (OBUs) interact with other entities within or
foreign to the domain through protocols like IEEE 802.11p or
cellular communication networks (such as 4G and 5G) to facil-
itate transportation and entertainment-related applications [5].
Due to the openness of such wireless communication channels,
security and privacy issues have arisen [6], [7], [8]. For exam-
ple, attackers can monitor, intercept, or tamper with real-time
messages in a vehicle communication channel, thereby posing a
threat to road trafﬁc safety or driver privacy. Furthermore, there
is a lack of trust between different domains. Therefore, when ve-
hicles access cross-domain services, the accessed domain must
authenticate the vehicle’s identity. Only after authentication can
a vehicle communicate with other entities within the domain
to ensure the security of the VANET communication system.
However, V2V and V2I communications across different trust
domains still face problems in terms of security authentication.
Currently, for the sake of manageability existing VANETs
cross-domain authentication solutions are limited to the same
or similar types of authentication or management techniques
in each domain [9], [10], or it is assumed that management
centers trust each other in advance. Many studies on secure
communication between cross-domain entities, such as cross-
domain vehicles and roadside units, are based on encryption
technologies, such as group signatures [11], [12] and anonymous
identity authentication [13], [14], [15]. They rely on centralized,
fully-trusted authorities to manage cross-domain vehicles [16],
[17], which increases an attacker’s determination to launch
attacks to compromise the private keys of the system and makes
it susceptible to single-point failures.

To address the shortcomings of centralized cross-domain
identity management schemes, recent decentralized VANET
cross-domain authentication solutions based on blockchain have
been proposed. For example, Yang et al. [18] introduced a multi-
domain authentication scheme for vehicular networks that en-
sures conditional privacy-preserving. They utilized blockchain
to facilitate cross-domain information sharing and designed
a pseudonym distribution mechanism to guarantee vehicle
anonymity and traceability. Li et al. [19] addressed the issue
of linkability in existing blockchain-based vehicular network
authentication schemes by proposing a collusion-resistant, un-
linkable authentication key protocol. This protocol achieves
multi-trust authority (TA) domain V2V and V2I communication
through different tickets, pseudonyms, and signatures.
However, even with the incorporation of blockchain technol-
ogy require third-party assistance in VANETs communication,
or these solutions face limitations inherent to the technology it-
self. For instance, communication entities within VANETs must
query the blockchain in real-time for mutual identity authenti-
cation, leading to increased communication latency that does
not meet the latency-sensitive requirements of highly mobile
vehicles. Additionally, cross-domain vehicles must ensure their
privacy, and therefore, they must communicate anonymously
to avoid being traced or attacked by malicious entities. While
protecting user privacy, there is also a need for trustworthy
authorities or distributed management systems capable of trac-
ing and revoking the identities of wrongdoers to ensure stable
operation of the entire vehicular network. Unfortunately, none
of the currently available cross-domain authentication solutions
for VANETs satisfy these requirements.
To address these limitations, we propose a blockchain-
assisted revocable cross-domain authentication solution for
VANETs. The scheme can support authentication between trust
domains running different identity management technologies
without changing existing intra-domain infrastructure deploy-
ments,particularlyforscenariosthatcrossborders.Furthermore,
to address the security and privacy issues of vehicle cross-
domain communication in VANETs, we employ a short random
group signature algorithm. This allows vehicles to generate
one-time licenses for communication with entities within the ac-
cessed domain, ensuring the integrity, anonymity, unlinkability,
and traceability of communicate messages. The contributions of
the proposed scheme are as follows:
1) To achieve decentralized authentication between entities
within different trust domains using different management
technologies, blockchain technology is used to distribute
information about each trust domain and cross-domain
vehicles without the need to assume prior mutual trust
between domain management centers. The accessed do-
main does not require additional storage for cross-domain
vehicle information. The vehicle only needs to complete
one round of interaction for authentication and obtain a
cross-domain license when it ﬁrst needs to cross domains,
which reduce the computational overhead.
2) To address the issue of conditional privacy-preserving in
cross-domain communication for VANETs, a short ran-
dom group signature method is used to enable vehicles to
self-generate one-time licenses for anonymous V2V and
V2I communication. During this process, no third-party
trusted authority or decentralized system is required to
assist. The accessed domain can trace the real identity of
a malicious vehicle and revoke its identity by updating the
domain key and smart contract design. The design of this
scheme avoids additional overhead caused by real-time
on-chain queries.
3) A comprehensive security analysis of the proposed
scheme was conducted, conﬁrming its security and condi-
tional privacy-preserving capabilities, particularly against
collusion attacks. Experimental simulation results show
that our scheme balances the computing and communi-
cation costs better than other cross-domain authentication
solutions for VANETs.

introduces related works in the ﬁeld. The system model is

the paper.
II. RELATED WORK
There have been a series of research work on authentication
design in different VANETs cross-domain scenarios, which are
mainly divided into centralized cross-domain authentication
schemesandblockchain-baseddistributedcross-domainauthen-
tication schemes.
In the centralized VANETs cross-domain authentication
scheme, a separate centralized server manages the identity of
cross-domain vehicles. Sun et al. [16] proposed a cross-domain
authorized secure data sharing scheme for autonomous vehicle
platoons. This scheme ensures secure communication between
two different platoons by designing a new ciphertext conversion
technology. However, the identity and privacy-preserving of
the leader PL is not considered. Lin et al. [17] proposed a
fog-based key agreement protocol and authentication scheme
among various entities. This framework facilitated cross-domain
access to other fog servers. But this scheme exhibited vulnera-
bilities to impersonation and man-in-the-middle attacks. Vilalta
Ricard et al. [20] made signiﬁcant contributions with a method
for vehicle message exchange within cross-border scenarios.
The proposed architecture offered valuable insights into the
exchange of messages between cross-border vehicles. Although
no speciﬁc design protocol is given, it provides readers with
a good research direction. Similarly, Chen et al. [21] intro-
duced a cloud storage-based scheme for inter-regional vehicle
anonymous authentication. This scheme effectively addressed
challenges related to the remote server’s inability to authenticate
vehicle identities. However, it did not offer provisions for track-
ing vehicle identities. Xu et al. [22] proposed a vehicle cross-
domain authentication scheme in LTE-A network based on ellip-
tic curve public key encryption algorithm, which can effectively
protect vehicle privacy and security. Yang et al. [23] proposed
an edge-assisted decentralized authentication architecture for

Lei et al. [3] proposed a key management scheme for Ve-

a secure message network to transmit and verify vehicle keys
for cross-border requests. However, this scheme considers the
mutual authentication of heterogeneous networks among differ-
ent Certiﬁcate Authorities (CAs). Yang et al. [24] introduced
a blockchain-based cross-domain authentication scheme for di-
verse IoT communication domains’ mobile devices within an
edge computing environment, achieving distributed manage-
ment of cross-domain identities. Xue et al. [25] presented a
distributed autonomous network cross-domain authentication
scheme in a multi-CA setting by utilizing blockchain to offer
a cross-domain certiﬁcate revocation mechanism. They decou-
ple on-chain and off-chain operations to mitigate the limita-
tions imposed by blockchain technology. Yang et al. [18] pro-
posed a multi-domain authentication solution for vehicular net-
works that focuses on privacy-preserving. This scheme employs
blockchain to enable cross-domain information sharing among
multiple management domains. However, it has pseudonym
linkability, requiring assistance from Roadside Units (RSUs)
and blockchain in V2V scenarios, with RSUs needing to be
completely trusted. Chen et al. [26] proposed an anonymous
cross-domain authentication scheme called XAuth based on
Public Key Infrastructure (PKI). This scheme integrates existing
PKI with a certiﬁcate transparency system and addresses the
limitations of blockchain technology by designing a lightweight
veriﬁcation protocol to enhance response speed. Hua et al. [27]
introduced a blockchain-based cross-domain self-authentication
scheme to address user identity storage in automated parking
systems. However, this scheme involves multiple rounds of
interaction between users and servers for authentication. Li
et al. [19] presented a collusion-resistant unlinkable VANETs
authentication key agreement by using blockchain technology
to realize V2V and V2R communication between multiple TA
domains through different tickets, different pseudonyms and
signatures. Xu et al. [28] designed a blockchain-based RSU-
assisted multi-TA cross-domain identity veriﬁcation and key
protocol. All TAs form a multi-server network to collectively
manage and store vehicle-related information. However, the
scheme requires RSUs to have tamper-resistant devices installed
and assumes that TAs do not collude with each other.
In summary, most existing research solutions are unable to
achieve cross-domain vehicle authentication between different
management domains for VANETs. Seamless switching dur-
ing cross-domain processes is unattainable, as cross-domain
identities are reliant on a single entity for management. Ad-
ditionally, issues persist with non-anonymous vehicle identi-
ties, pseudonym linkability, and the inability to trace and re-
voke malicious identities. Furthermore, solutions incorporating
blockchain technology are constrained by the limitations of the
technology itself or require third-party auxiliary communica-
tion, which fail to meet the latency-sensitive requirements of
VANETs. Therefore, it is necessary to design a decentralized
and revocable cross-domain authentication solution that meets
the requirement for low-latency communication to ensure the
security of VANETs. Blockchain is solely utilized for managing
cross-domain identities and does not participate in real-time
communication processes, thereby avoiding the impact of the
technology’s inherent high conﬁrmation latency.

B. System Model
Entities involved in the proposed scheme are the original do-
main authentication server (OAS), roadside unit (RSU), vehicle
and dedicated server (DS), as shown in Fig. 1. The entities and
their responsibilities are described as follows.
r OAS: Original domain authentication server (OAS) refers
to the role responsible for managing the identities of ve-
hicles, infrastructure, and other entities within different
management domains. For example, it could be a CA
managing device certiﬁcates in a certiﬁcate-based authen-
tication scheme, a Key Generation Center (KGC) in an
identity-based authentication scheme, or a TA in other
authentication schemes. OAS is considered trustworthy
for all entities within its respective management domain.
However, there could be a risk of collusion with other OAS
entities.
r RSU: RSUs within each management domain form a con-
sortium blockchain, providing vehicles with an API to
invoke smart contract functions and assisting vehicles to
updating relevant licenses. It is assumed that these RSUs
will not provide erroneous APIs.
r Vehicle: Vehicles are equipped with an OBU and a Tamper-
Proof Device (TPD) [26]. OBUs facilitate V2V and V2I
communication with other vehicles or roadside infrastruc-
ture. TPDs are used to securely store materials and compute
secret values. In VANETs, vehicles are considered un-
trusted entities, hence messages originating from vehicles
require identity veriﬁcation.
r DS: Each management domain’s OAS selects a DS for
cross-domain authentication. All DSs form a consortium
blockchain, which offer an API for participating domain’s
OAS to invoke smart contract functions and aid in the dy-
namic management of cross-domain vehicle information.

D. Threat Model
In the proposed scheme, vehicles communicate with entities
in multiple management domains through wireless channels,
which exposes them to various common attacks in VANETs.
According to the famous Dolev-Yao threat model [32], attacks
launched by attackers can be classiﬁed into passive and active
attacks, including eavesdropping, tampering, impersonation, re-
play, and man-in-the-middle attacks. In addition, there is a risk
of server collusion in multi-domain scenarios. Server collusion
refers to the collusion between servers in multiple administrative
domains to interfere with the normal access and communication
of vehicles in another domain or leak their private data. For
example, in cross-domain authentication, the authority of the
accessed domain may mark the real identity of the cross-domain
vehicles, capture their cross-domain access records, and thereby
expose the location privacy of the vehicles. Therefore, OASs in
each domain do not always trust each other.

B. Vehicle Intra-Domain Authentication
In this phase, the proposed scheme does not require entities
managed within the home domain (such as vehicles or RSUs)
to modify their existing authentication schemes. This means
that entities within the home domain can continue using their
original authentication methods for communication. In contrast
to approaches like [18], [26], [19] and [25], which necessitate a
uniﬁed re-registration and authentication process for vehicles in
a multi-domain scenario, our scheme signiﬁcantly reduces the
time cost associated with implementing a uniﬁed authentication
technology.

VII. CONCLUSION
To address the security and privacy problems in cross-
domain authentication for VANETs, we proposed a revocable
cross-domain authentication scheme based on blockchain to
protectvehiclecommunication.Ourschemeallowssecurecross-
domain authentication between domains using different man-
agement technologies, which can be completed in only one
round of interaction. Cross-domain identities are distributed and
managed by smart contracts deployed on a blockchain system,
thereby reducing the likelihood of a single point of failure.
Additionally, in the V2V and V2I communication processes, the
short random signature method is used to protect the identity
privacy of the cross-domain vehicle, and the legitimate rights
and interests of the accessed domain are guaranteed by revoking
the identity of malicious vehicles. This scheme avoids real-time
on-chain queries to meet the latency-sensitive requirements of
VANETs. Security and performance analyses show that the
proposed scheme meets the basic security and privacy require-
ments of VANETs and has certain advantages over the most
advanced cross-domain authentication schemes for VANETs.
In the future, we will further optimize the computing overhead
of cross-domain communication and improve the overall perfor-
mance in cross-domain scenarios.
